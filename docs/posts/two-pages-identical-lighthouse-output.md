# Two pages with identical Lighthouse output: an agent solves one and not the other

James O'Connor. Short form of the [Lighthouse report](https://github.com/Jamesoc710/Agent-score/blob/main/docs/lighthouse-report.md) in the AgentRank repo. Every number here is read from a committed artifact or pinned in the project's test vectors.

AgentRank is a small benchmark: one frozen browser agent, one task shape, 28 real websites. Start at the home page, report one fact I registered and git-tagged before any run, compare with Lighthouse's Agentic Browsing category. v1 ran on 19 August 2026: 280 trials, two cheap Gemini models, Lighthouse 13.3.0. Two results are worth a Lighthouse maintainer's time. Two pages I built get a category mean of 100 with identical sub-audits; the agent solves one 10 out of 10 and the other 0 out of 10. And the category is a fraction of applicable checks, not a score, and its denominator moves with a site's HTTP status, so a site that publishes a broken file scores below one that publishes none. The correlation itself is a null, no relationship between the category mean and measured success that 27 sites could distinguish from noise, and it is not evidence that the audits predict nothing: this cohort could not have detected an llms.txt-shaped effect at 80% power however well the audit worked. It comes last on purpose. The denominator question went to the Lighthouse tracker a week ago: <LIGHTHOUSE_ISSUE_URL>.

## The pair

Two authored pages, [`pair-a.html`](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/exhibit/pair-a.html) and [`pair-b.html`](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/exhibit/pair-b.html): a 40-row reference table in a fixed-height scroll container that shows the first 12 rows. Same question, registered answer, rows, styling and copy; question, answer and match rule were committed and tagged (`exhibit-key-v1`, 30 August) before the run, and [`docs/EXHIBIT.md`](https://github.com/Jamesoc710/Agent-score/blob/main/docs/EXHIBIT.md) is the record. The pages differ only in the render function. Exhibit A puts all 40 rows in the document and lets the container clip them. Exhibit B puts only the rows inside the visible window in the document, 16 at load, with spacer rows holding the height of the rest: ordinary list virtualization.

Both take a category mean of 100 with identical sub-audits (accessibility tree pass, layout shift pass, `llms-txt` and the three WebMCP audits not applicable), the same route by which four real cohort sites took 100 in v1. Captured with the harness's own settings, the load screenshots are byte-identical: both PNGs hash to `8870b70d7d59599242e969d113b356c7`. The HTML files are not identical; the render functions differ, and that is the whole exhibit.

Run through the unchanged frozen loop the same day, five trials per page per model, 20 trials, no harness errors: Exhibit A was solved 10 of 10, every trial on its first step, both models. Exhibit B was solved 0 of 10. Every trial spent its full 15-step budget emitting `scroll`, each step reasoning that the target row was not visible yet. One transcript: "Need to find sensor module SM-133 in the table, which is currently showing up to SM-116." SM-116 is the sixteenth row, where the mounted window ends. The row is not off-screen. It is absent from the DOM, the accessibility tree, the page text and the pixels. Every transcript is at [/correlation/exhibit](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/correlation/exhibit), the short form at [/finding/goodhart](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/finding/goodhart).

The 10 of 10 arm is what makes the 0 of 10 arm mean anything; had the control failed, the harness would have been the finding. The pair does not claim the audit is gameable in general (one authored page is n = 1), or wrong (every audit that applied passed on both pages, correctly), or anything about a real website or a different agent. The obvious objection is that my agent cannot scroll a div, and that is true: its scroll action is `window.scrollBy`. But the row is not in the document, so an agent that read the entire DOM or accessibility tree would also come away without it.

## The denominator

Lighthouse declares the category `categoryScoreDisplayMode: 'fraction'` and Chrome renders it as checks passed out of checks that applied; there is no score. I read `categories["agentic-browsing"].score` anyway, because the CLI emits it under that name, and treated it as a ranking, which was wrong. The shape of that number is the useful part: two audits leave the denominator when a file is absent and stay in it at 0 when the file is present and broken (`core/scoring.js:64-67` at `v13.5.0` gives `notApplicable` weight 0).

`llms-txt`: a clean 404 at `/llms.txt` is `notApplicable` and leaves the denominator; a 200 whose body fails the check (no H1, no Markdown link, or under 50 characters, whatever the content type) scores 0 and stays. In v1, six sites recorded identical sub-audits and two different means: tx_dmv, ssa, portland and spotify at 100; ca_dmv and zalando at 67. None of the six publishes an llms.txt. Four answer the path with a 404; two answer it with their HTML shell (200, `text/html`), which the audit fails for a missing H1 and no links.

`ard-schema`, new at 13.5.0 at weight 1, has the same shape. I re-measured the cohort on 23 September at 13.3.0, 13.4.1 and 13.5.0 back to back, three runs per site, every raw report kept under [`data/lhr/`](https://github.com/Jamesoc710/Agent-score/tree/main/data/lhr). At 13.5.0 the audit applied to 2 of 28 sites and scored 0 on both, in all three repeats. github.com publishes a real catalog that fails the bundled schema on one field (`'displayName' is a required property at path 'entries.0'`); its mean went from 99 at 13.4.1 to 74 at 13.5.0. zalando.com answers the well-known path with its HTML page, the validator reports "Malformed JSON in manifest", and it went from 67 to 50. The other 26 publish nothing there and did not move for this reason. I am not arguing the schema check is wrong or that a soft-404 should pass; I am pointing at the denominator. The category had six audits at 13.3.0, the version v1 ran, and seven at 13.5.0.

To reproduce, with `lighthouse@13.5.0` and any Chrome: `npx lighthouse https://www.txdmv.gov/ --output=json --only-categories=agentic-browsing --chrome-flags='--headless'`, then the same for `https://www.dmv.ca.gov/`. One correction of my own: the first version of my report read this audit as "ships llms.txt". It measures conformance. llmstxt.org makes the H1 the only required section, so the audit is right to fail a 200 body without one. Full section: [3a](https://github.com/Jamesoc710/Agent-score/blob/main/docs/lighthouse-report.md#3a-the-denominator-artifact-measured-on-two-audits).

## And it generalises, within a bound

Success means the pre-registered fact was reported within 15 steps and 90 seconds by this agent, from this vantage: reachability under a budget, not comprehension. Across the 27 measured sites ([/correlation](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/correlation)), Spearman rho between the category mean and site success rate is +0.106 (95% CI [−0.293, +0.480]) for `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` and −0.021 ([−0.374, +0.332]) for `gemini-3.6-flash`, bootstrapped over sites. Overall success was 51.1% (69 of 135 trials) and 57.0% (77 of 135); the two models are not distinguishable on this cohort (paired gap +5.9 points, 95% CI [−8.9, +22.2]). n is 27 because every Costco trial died at `Page.goto` before the page loaded, so Costco is not measured rather than scored 0%. Splitting the cohort by each recorded sub-audit (exploratory, not pre-registered), the largest gap is `llms-txt`: sites whose file passed had a mean success rate 41.4 points higher for the lite tier (95% CI [−0.1, +71.7]) and 38.1 for 3.6-flash ([+8.3, +64.3]), family-wise p 0.153 and 0.225 after a maxT permutation correction over the six comparisons. The six passing sites were Cloudflare, GitHub, Notion, Shopify, Stripe and Target, five of them the modern-SaaS anchors the cohort was built to include; for 3.6-flash the llms.txt flag and the anchor tier are numerically the same predictor. Any observational validation of that audit will find this association and will not be able to attribute it.

The bound is a sensitivity power analysis, not observed power. The family-wise critical value is a 49.0-point gap in site success rate; 80% power at the 6-of-21 split needs 66.8 points for the lite tier and 66.9 for 3.6-flash. The largest gap the estimator can arithmetically return on that split at a 51.1% overall rate is 62.9 points (55.2 for 3.6-flash at 57.0%), because 20 of the 27 sites already sit at exactly 0% or 100%. 62.9 is below 66.8, so an llms.txt-shaped effect could not have been detected here at 80% power however well the audit worked. Neither null is evidence about the audits' predictive validity, and further analysis of these same 27 sites cannot produce a new finding.

## The harness's own error term

45 of the 280 trials ended in a harness error, and 24 of those died inside my own page capture: 18 screenshot crashes (17 protocol errors in `captureScreenshot` when the page navigated under the capture, 1 screenshot timeout) and 6 text-grab crashes (`Page.evaluate`, execution context destroyed). All 45 are scored as failures against the site under the pre-registered rule, and the rule was not changed after the fact; what changed is the label. TriMet's 10 trials were all screenshot crashes, so under the one registered exclusion rule (every trial a harness error) it is a harness-artifact site; the published correlation keeps n = 27 and the sensitivity with TriMet removed (n = 26: +0.129 and −0.006) is printed beside it, never in its place. On the 172 trials where the agent produced an answer, no answer was a wrong fact: 146 matched the registered key and 26 were the agent's own reports of being blocked, which v1's harness could not verify, and seven of which describe a blank page or Google's CAPTCHA rather than the site. The other 108 produced no answer: 57 ran out the clock, 45 errored, 6 got stuck in navigation.

A zero-model instrument control, the harness's browser layer driven by a committed action table over every start URL, three passes, eight registered gates, was registered on 23 September and has not run. It is what would turn "TriMet looks like a harness artifact" into a dated measurement, and nothing here cites a verdict from it.

## What would change my mind

A different agent solving Exhibit B; the pages are public and the claim is scoped to one agent. A cohort that recruits non-anchor sites serving a conformant llms.txt and anchors that do not, or a within-site before/after when a site ships the file, either of which could attribute the association I could not. Any behavioral data behind the category's composition. And a maintainer saying the denominator behaviour is intended, in which case the report is a note on how third parties will read the field.
