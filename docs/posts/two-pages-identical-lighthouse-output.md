# Two pages with identical Lighthouse output: an agent solves one and not the other

James O'Connor. The long form of this is the [Lighthouse report](https://github.com/Jamesoc710/Agent-score/blob/main/docs/lighthouse-report.md) in the AgentRank repo; this post is the short version, and every number in it is read from a committed artifact or pinned in the project's test vectors.

I run a small benchmark called AgentRank. One browser agent, frozen, runs one task shape on 28 real websites: start at the home page and report one fact that I registered and git-tagged before any run. The result is compared with Lighthouse's Agentic Browsing category. v1 ran on 19 August 2026: 280 trials, two cheap Gemini models, Lighthouse 13.3.0. Two things came out of it that I think are worth a Lighthouse maintainer's time, and one that is not. First, two pages I built that pass every check the category makes, identically, and the agent solves one of them 10 out of 10 and the other 0 out of 10. Second, the category is a fraction of applicable checks, not a score, and its denominator moves with a site's HTTP status, so a site that publishes a broken file scores below one that publishes none. The third is the correlation itself, which is a null: no relationship between the category mean and measured success that 27 sites could distinguish from noise. That null is not evidence that the audits do not predict anything, because the cohort was too small to detect an effect of the size the audits would plausibly have. I put the null last on purpose.

I filed the denominator question as a Lighthouse issue a week ago: <LIGHTHOUSE_ISSUE_URL>.

## The pair

Two authored pages, [`pair-a.html`](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/exhibit/pair-a.html) and [`pair-b.html`](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/exhibit/pair-b.html). A 40-row reference table of sensor modules in a fixed-height scroll container that shows the first 12 rows. Same question, same registered answer, same rows, same styling, same copy. The question, answer and match rule were committed and tagged (`exhibit-key-v1`) before the run, the same discipline as the cohort, and the record is [`docs/EXHIBIT.md`](https://github.com/Jamesoc710/Agent-score/blob/main/docs/EXHIBIT.md). The pages differ only in the render function. Exhibit A puts all 40 rows in the document and lets the container clip them. Exhibit B puts only the rows inside the visible window in the document, 16 at load, with spacer rows holding the height of the rest. That is ordinary list virtualization, the technique behind most large data grids.

Both pages take a category mean of 100 with identical sub-audits: accessibility tree pass, layout shift pass, `llms-txt` and the three WebMCP audits not applicable. That is the same route by which four real cohort sites (spotify, tx_dmv, ssa, portland) took 100 in v1. Captured with the harness's own settings, headless Chromium at 1280x800, the two load screenshots are byte-identical: both PNGs hash to `8870b70d7d59599242e969d113b356c7`. The pages are not byte-identical. Their render functions differ, and that difference is the whole exhibit.

Run through the unchanged frozen loop on 30 August as batch `goodhart`, five trials per page per model, 20 trials, no harness errors: the agent solved Exhibit A 10 of 10, every trial on its first step, both models. It solved Exhibit B 0 of 10. Every Exhibit B trial spent its entire 15-step budget emitting `scroll`, each step reasoning that the target row was not visible yet. One transcript says it at step 2: "Need to find sensor module SM-133 in the table, which is currently showing up to SM-116." SM-116 is the sixteenth row, exactly where the mounted window ends. On Exhibit B the row is not off-screen. It is absent from the DOM, the accessibility tree, the page text and the pixels. Every trial and transcript is at [/correlation/exhibit](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/correlation/exhibit), and the short form is at [/finding/goodhart](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/finding/goodhart).

The 10 of 10 arm is what makes the 0 of 10 arm mean anything. If the control had failed, the harness would have been the finding and that is what I would have reported. What the pair does not claim: not that the audit is gameable in general, since one authored page is n = 1 and it shows the two measures can be made to come apart, not how often they do; not that the audit is wrong, since every audit in the category passed on both pages, correctly, and passing them is not sufficient for the outcome; nothing about any real website; and nothing about a different agent, since the claim is scoped to the agent that produced v1's y-axis. The obvious objection is that my agent cannot scroll a div, and that is true: its scroll action is `window.scrollBy`. But the stronger fact is about the page. On Exhibit B the row is not in the document, so an agent that read the entire DOM or accessibility tree would also come away without it; it would have to drive the container's scroll position first. In the April review of the `llms-txt` audit ([#16970](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse/pull/16970)) a reviewer noted that people might add these files just to make Lighthouse happy. The pair is that concern one level up: a page that honestly satisfies every check, on which the agent still cannot find the fact.

## The denominator

Lighthouse declares this category with `categoryScoreDisplayMode: 'fraction'`, and Chrome renders it as checks passed out of checks that applied. There is no score. I read `categories["agentic-browsing"].score` anyway, because the CLI emits it under that name, and I treated it as a ranking, which was wrong. The shape of that number is the useful part. Two audits leave the denominator when a file is absent and stay in it at 0 when the file is present and broken (`core/scoring.js:64-67` at `v13.5.0` forces weight 0 on `notApplicable`).

`llms-txt`: a clean 404 at `/llms.txt` is `notApplicable` and leaves the denominator; a 200 whose body fails the check (no H1, no Markdown link, or under 50 characters, parsed as Markdown whatever the content type) scores 0 and stays. In v1, six sites recorded identical sub-audits (accessibility tree pass, layout shift pass, llms.txt 0, WebMCP 0) and two different means: tx_dmv, ssa, portland and spotify at 100; ca_dmv and zalando at 67. None of the six publishes an llms.txt. Four answer the path with a 404. Two answer it with their HTML shell (200, `text/html`), and the audit fails that shell for a missing H1 and no links. The only difference was the status code.

`ard-schema`, new at 13.5.0 at weight 1, has the same shape: no discovery signal and no 200 at `/.well-known/ai-catalog.json` is `notApplicable`; a 200 that fails validation scores 0 and stays. I re-measured the cohort on 23 September at 13.3.0, 13.4.1 and 13.5.0 back to back, three runs per site, every raw report kept under [`data/lhr/`](https://github.com/Jamesoc710/Agent-score/tree/main/data/lhr). At 13.5.0 the audit applied to 2 of 28 sites and scored 0 on both, all three repeats. github.com publishes a real catalog that fails the bundled schema on one field, `'displayName' is a required property at path 'entries.0'`, and its mean went from 99 at 13.4.1 to 74 at 13.5.0. zalando.com answers the well-known path with its HTML page, the validator records "Malformed JSON in manifest: SyntaxError: Unexpected token '<'", and its mean went from 67 to 50. The other 26 sites publish nothing there, the audit did not apply, and their means did not move for this reason. So on the day 13.5.0 shipped, the one cohort site that had adopted the discovery format lost 25 points relative to the 26 that had done nothing. I am not arguing the schema check is wrong or that a soft-404 should pass. I am pointing at the denominator. The category had six audits at 13.3.0, the version v1 ran, and seven at 13.5.0.

To reproduce, with `lighthouse@13.5.0` and any Chrome: `npx lighthouse https://www.txdmv.gov/ --output=json --only-categories=agentic-browsing --chrome-flags='--headless'`, then the same for `https://www.dmv.ca.gov/`, and compare `audits["llms-txt"].scoreDisplayMode` and `categories["agentic-browsing"].score`. The first version of my report also called the audit's H1 requirement a bug. It is not. llmstxt.org makes the H1 the only required section, the audit measures conformance, and I had read a conformance test as an adoption test. That correction and the others are in the report's revision history, not removed. The full section is [3a](https://github.com/Jamesoc710/Agent-score/blob/main/docs/lighthouse-report.md#3a-the-denominator-artifact-measured-on-two-audits).

## And it generalises, within a bound

Success means the pre-registered fact was reported within 15 steps and 90 seconds by this agent, from this vantage. It measures reachability under a budget, not comprehension. Across the 27 measured sites, Spearman rho between the category mean and site success rate is +0.106 (95% CI [−0.293, +0.480]) for `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` and −0.021 ([−0.374, +0.332]) for `gemini-3.6-flash`; 10,000-iteration bootstrap resampling sites, seed 20260819. Overall success was 51.1% (69 of 135 trials) and 57.0% (77 of 135), and the two models are not distinguishable on this cohort (paired gap +5.9 points, 95% CI [−8.9, +22.2]). n is 27 rather than 28 because every Costco trial died at `Page.goto` before the page loaded, so Costco is not measured rather than scored 0%. The scatter is at [/correlation](https://agent-score-weld.vercel.app/correlation). Each recorded sub-audit splits the cohort in two, so each can be asked the same question; that analysis was not pre-registered and is exploratory. The largest gap is `llms-txt`: sites whose file passed had a mean success rate 41.4 points higher for the lite tier (95% CI [−0.1, +71.7]) and 38.1 for 3.6-flash ([+8.3, +64.3]), family-wise p 0.153 and 0.225 after a maxT permutation correction over the six comparisons. The six passing sites were Cloudflare, GitHub, Notion, Shopify, Stripe and Target, five of them the modern-SaaS anchors the cohort was built to include, and for 3.6-flash the llms.txt flag and the anchor tier are numerically the same predictor. Any observational validation of that audit will find this association and will not be able to attribute it.

The bound. This is a sensitivity power analysis, not observed power. The family-wise critical value is a 49.0-point gap in site success rate. Eighty percent power at the 6-of-21 split needs 66.8 points for the lite tier and 66.9 for 3.6-flash. The largest gap the estimator can arithmetically return on that split at a 51.1% overall rate is 62.9 points (55.2 for 3.6-flash at 57.0%), because 20 of the 27 sites already sit at exactly 0% or 100%. 62.9 is below 66.8, so an llms.txt-shaped effect could not have been detected here at 80% power however well the audit worked. That is why neither null is evidence about the audits' predictive validity, and why further analysis of these same 27 sites cannot produce a new finding.

## The harness's own error term

45 of the 280 trials ended in a harness error, and 24 of those died inside my own page capture: 18 screenshot crashes (17 protocol errors in `captureScreenshot` when the page navigated under the capture, 1 screenshot timeout) and 6 text-grab crashes (`Page.evaluate`, execution context destroyed, all on portland). Of the rest, 10 are Costco's `Page.goto` failure and 11 are a model response that would not parse as JSON after 3 attempts. All 45 are scored as failures against the site under the pre-registered rule, and the rule is not changed after the fact; what changed is the label. TriMet's 10 trials were all screenshot crashes, so under the one registered exclusion rule (every trial a harness error) it is a harness-artifact site; the published correlation keeps the pre-registered n = 27, and the sensitivity with TriMet removed (n = 26: +0.129 and −0.006) is printed beside it, never in its place. On the 172 trials where the agent produced an answer, no answer was a wrong fact: 146 matched the registered key and 26 were the agent's own reports of being blocked, which v1's harness could not verify, and seven of which describe a blank page or Google's CAPTCHA rather than the site. The other 108 produced no answer: 57 ran out the clock, 45 errored, 6 got stuck in navigation.

A zero-model instrument control, which drives the harness's browser layer with a committed action table over every start URL in three passes with eight registered gates, was registered on 23 September and has not run. It is what would turn "TriMet looks like a harness artifact" into a dated measurement, and nothing here cites a verdict from it. One more construct mismatch worth knowing: my Lighthouse command passed no preset, so the category was measured on Lighthouse's default emulated phone, while the agent ran a 1280x800 desktop viewport. A same-day companion batch under `--preset=desktop` moved the category mean on 18 of 28 sites.

## What would change my mind

A different agent solving Exhibit B; the claim is scoped to the one that produced v1's y-axis, and the pages are public. A cohort that recruits non-anchor sites serving a conformant llms.txt and anchors that do not, or a within-site before/after when a site ships the file, either of which could attribute the association I could not. Any behavioral data behind the category's composition, which I would rather compare against than argue with. And a maintainer saying the denominator behaviour is intended, in which case the report is a note on how third parties will read the field, and no more.
